img3-Exudative tonsillopharyngitis
Pharynx and tonsils in a patient with infectious mononucleosis
The tonsils are massively hypertrophied, touching at the midline (known as “kissing tonsils”), and covered with gray-white exudate. The visible parts of the pharynx are erythematous.

img4-Angular cheilitis
Multiple linear cracks of the epidermis (circled), approx. 0.5 cm in length, are located at the corner of the mouth. The occurrence of these fissures is called angular cheilitis and has many possible causes, e.g., iron deficiency, mechanical irritation, or infection.

img5-Atypical lymphocytosis in infectious mononucleosis
Photomicrograph of a peripheral blood smear (Wright-Giemsa stain; very high magnification)
Multiple atypical lymphocytes (1) are visible. Atypical lymphocytes are activated T cells with a polymorphic nucleus and abundant basophilic cytoplasm. Inactive lymphocytes (2) are comparatively smaller with a round nucleus and thin cytoplasm rim. Despite being activated T cells, atypical lymphocytes strongly resemble monocytes (3), hence the name infectious mononucleosis.

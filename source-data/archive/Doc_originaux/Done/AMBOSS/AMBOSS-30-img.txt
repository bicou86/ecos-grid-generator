img2-Exudative tonsillopharyngitis
Pharynx and tonsils in a patient with infectious mononucleosis
The tonsils are massively hypertrophied, touching at the midline (known as “kissing tonsils”), and covered with gray-white exudate. The visible parts of the pharynx are erythematous.

img3-Acute pharyngitis
Multiple dilated blood vessels are visible on the posterior pharyngeal wall, anterior tonsillar pillars, and the uvula. Lymphoid hyperplasia is visible, identifiable as multiple small nodules on the pharyngeal mucosa. The tonsils are not enlarged.
These are the typical features of acute pharyngitis, which is most commonly caused by a viral infection.
